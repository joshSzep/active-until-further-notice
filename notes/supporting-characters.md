# Supporting Characters

## Four distinct relationships to Later Her — established

Four different people make Later Her difficult for the waking protagonist to reconcile with herself. These are separate characters, not alternative roles for one person. The person who loves her is her adult child. Their names, further relationship details, histories, and order of introduction remain open.

### The person who loves her — her adult child

Her adult child approaches with love, intimacy, and trust grounded in a relationship with Later Her. The child was born after the persistence state from which Earlier Her wakes. Earlier Her has never experienced having or raising this person; the child's entire life belongs to the history she does not remember.

The child understands persistence and the distinction between the two versions. Their emotional response does not depend on mistaking Earlier Her for the mother whose shared life they remember.

The child's primary hope is that Earlier Her will help recover Later Her. They care about Earlier Her, but desperately want their mother back: the woman with whom they shared their life. How directly they express this hope, and how it affects their developing relationship with Earlier Her, remain open.

The child's name, gender, age, other parent or family structure, and political views remain open. Being the person who loves her does not establish that their relationship with Later Her was uncomplicated. Wanting their mother restored does not establish indifference to Earlier Her.

### The person who fears her

An ordinary interaction reveals that Later Her held frightening power. The source of that power and the events behind this person's fear remain open.

### The person who blames her

This person was harmed by a decision Later Her made, a decision Earlier Her cannot imagine making. The act, circumstances, and distribution of responsibility remain open.

### The person who believes in her

A former revolutionary expects conviction and courage that Earlier Her does not recognize in herself. Their exact relationship to Later Her and present political circumstances remain open.

## Development questions

- Which person does she meet first?
- How does the adult child express their wish for Earlier Her to recover Later Her?
- How does their care for Earlier Her interact with their stronger wish to have their mother back?
- What specific shared history grounds each response?
- How does each person distinguish Earlier Her from Later Her in practice?
- What does each want from her now?
- How do their relationships with Earlier Her change as she builds her own life?

The four responses establish different encounters with Later Her's legacy. They do not yet settle her exact revolutionary rank, whether the accounts conflict factually, or whether any of these people is misleading the protagonist.
