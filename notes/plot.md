# Plot

## Decided catastrophe premise

Recent persistence states were deliberately made inaccessible as both a protective measure and a political act. Someone sealed them away to prevent a danger, and denying access to those people was also a means of changing who controls the ship.

Recovery may undo that protection. The nature of the danger, who acted, whose power they sought to change, and whether the action was justified remain unresolved. The later protagonist's involvement is not yet decided.

## Current spine

This is a working plot shape, not locked canon.

### Act I: Waking

The protagonist is instantiated from an old but valid persistence state after a long dormancy.

She learns that:

- decades have passed
- more recent persistence states are inaccessible
- thousands of people may be affected
- her own later state is among the inaccessible
- she has been designated **Active Until Further Notice**

She is told she is uniquely valuable to current mission objectives.

The designation is unsettling because indefinite embodiment is rare and resource-expensive.

### Act II: Recovery

The protagonist begins trying to recover the inaccessible persistence infrastructure.

As she works, she learns what happened during the decades she does not remember.

People around her knew later versions of her.

Her own history becomes almost archaeological.

The technical recovery problem and the identity problem should increasingly become the same problem.

The protagonist may discover:

- relationships she does not remember
- choices she would not currently make
- a reputation she does not recognize
- disagreements inside The Board about whether she should have been awakened
- evidence that the later version of herself understood the catastrophe differently

### Act III: Branching

Recovery succeeds or becomes possible.

The later persistence state can potentially be instantiated.

By now the protagonist no longer experiences that later state simply as "herself."

The ship may now have two legitimate continuations of one identity:

- the later archived version, carrying decades of lost history
- the currently embodied protagonist, carrying the new life formed since waking

The Board may regard this as a resource-allocation problem rather than a metaphysical crisis.

Human society does not have the luxury of treating it so cleanly.

## Central dramatic reversal

At the beginning:

> Recovering the later version means getting myself back.

Later:

> Recovering the later version may mean creating another person whose claims to my life are stronger than mine.

## Potential stakes

The catastrophe should affect more than the protagonist.

Recovering the archive may restore access to thousands of lost persistence states.

That gives the protagonist a moral reason to continue even if success threatens her own identity or embodiment allocation.

## Not yet settled

- who deliberately sealed the persistence states away and how
- what danger the sealing was intended to prevent
- how denying access to those people was meant to change control of the ship
- whether the later protagonist contributed to it
- why the old version is uniquely capable of repairing it
- whether restoring the archive itself creates new danger
- whether both versions can legally be embodied at once
- what The Board ultimately recommends
- the ending
