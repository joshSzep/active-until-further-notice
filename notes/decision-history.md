# Decision History

This log records decisions made through the question-and-answer development process. Each decision is committed separately. Unchosen details remain open.

## 2026-09-25 — 001: Protective and political catastrophe

**Question:** What kind of catastrophe made the recent persistence states inaccessible?

**Options offered:** Technical failure; deliberate protection; political act; an intervention gone wrong.

**Author's answer:** "2 + 3" — deliberate protection and political act.

**Decision:** Someone deliberately sealed the persistence states away to prevent a danger. Denying access to those people was also a means of changing who controls the ship. Restoring access may undo that protection.

**Reasoning:** The author selected the combined direction without giving further rationale. The protective and political purposes are both part of the premise; their relationship remains to be developed.

**Still open:** The actor, mechanism, danger, intended shift in power, justification, and later protagonist's involvement.

## 2026-09-25 — 002: Explore the reintroduction of death

**Question:** Who were they trying to protect, and from whom?

**Options offered:** Dormant people from The Board; the ship from a returning faction; new generations from entrenched founders; everyone from concentrated archive control.

**Author's response:** None of these options currently fit. The author instead asked who benefits from the ability to resurrect increasingly old persistence states but not recent people, observing: "It's as if 'death' is being re-introduced into society".

**Direction:** Explore the loss of recent versions as a return of mortality, even while older versions remain recoverable. Investigate beneficiaries through that consequence before choosing a faction or perpetrator.

**Status:** Exploratory. No beneficiary or specific motive has been chosen. The earlier protective-and-political premise remains in place; its meaning is still being developed.

**Still open:** Whether the loss is progressive or follows a fixed cutoff; whether new states can be persisted and restored; what happens to people with no sufficiently old state; whether renewed mortality is the purpose or a consequence.
