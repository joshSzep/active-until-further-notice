# Persistence

## Canon

Persistence technology predates the generation ship and is already mature enough to shape ordinary life on Earth.

A person's mental state is persisted **daily during sleep**.

Under normal access conditions, most accidental biological death costs at most part of a day of memory. The person can be reconstructed from the most recent valid persistence state. The ship's suppression policy interrupts access to restoration, not nightly capture.

Persistence does not make bodies immortal. Bodies remain expensive, fragile biological systems. A person can be stored cheaply while dormant and instantiated into a body when needed or permitted.

The key distinction aboard ship is:

- **Persistence age** can be only hours old.
- **Instantiation gap** can be decades.

Someone may go to sleep, persist successfully, die shortly afterward, remain dormant for sixty years, and then wake subjectively feeling as though only one night passed.

## Social consequences

A mature persistence society would treat biological death and terminal loss as different categories.

Likely consequences include:

- "When was their last persistence?" becomes an ordinary question after accidents.
- Dangerous procedures may be preceded by an emergency persistence.
- Long periods without persistence increase continuity risk.
- Losing persistence infrastructure is vastly more serious than ordinary data loss.
- People can grieve lost periods of another person's life even if that person is restored.
- Memory gaps after restoration can create strange relationship asymmetries.

A spouse might remember events shared with a version who no longer remembers them.

## Identity

The practical workings of persistence and branching are common knowledge in this society. The protagonist understands from the outset that restoring a later state alongside her would produce a separate person; restoration does not mean absorbing that person's memories or becoming whole. This practical understanding leaves the philosophical questions below unresolved.

Society probably had to operationalize the identity question long before the ship launched.

The practical legal answer is likely that a validated continuity chain is treated as the same legal identity even if the metaphysical question remains unsettled.

Accounts, contracts, citizenship, debts, marriage, property, and responsibility can follow the identity lineage rather than a specific body.

The technology does not actually prove that continuity of consciousness survives reconstruction. Civilization has simply learned to function despite that uncertainty.

## Branching

If two valid versions of one persisted identity can exist simultaneously, identity can branch.

That raises difficult questions:

- Which version owns shared property?
- Which version remains married?
- Which version inherits prior obligations?
- When does one legal identity fork into two?

The novel should eventually decide how Earth society normally handles branching, but this is not settled yet.

## Restricted archive and selective retrieval

During suppression of the ship's revolution, The Board deliberately removed ordinary administrative access to the revolutionary archive to make the restriction durable. It retains the ability to reconnect the archive broadly but cannot selectively recover individuals through the remaining access.

Earlier Her's assignment is to design a new mechanism for selective retrieval. Later Her understood the revolutionary persistence architecture; her precise role in its development remains open.

Broad reconnection would restore reliable persistence for everyone living aboard. This would remove the fear of permanent loss that The Board uses to enforce compliance. It therefore seeks selective recovery without restoring that general guarantee.

Nightly persistence continues during the restriction. New states are captured and retained behind the seal, but cannot be restored through the available access. Broad reconnection could recover the missing years as well as restore reliable recovery for future deaths.

Continued preservation and deliberate denial of restoration are public knowledge. The technical possibility of broad reconnection is not. The Board publicly justifies the restriction as necessary for mission survival.

The later selves are preserved rather than automatically deleted or overwritten. The fear of permanent loss concerns their potentially indefinite exclusion from embodied life under continued restriction; it is not proof that their information has been destroyed. Exact historical retention policies remain open.

The technical form of the removed access, continued writes into the sealed archive, and the new retrieval mechanism are undecided. Reconnection's chosen political consequence is restored reliable persistence; independent retrieval and renewed autonomy of revolutionary infrastructure were not selected. It does not establish automatic embodiment of the archive's population.

## Ship-specific pressure

Persistence is easy compared with embodiment.

The ship can retain far more persisted people than it can keep embodied.

This turns persistence into infrastructure for population management rather than merely disaster recovery.

The crucial shipboard distinction is therefore not simply alive/dead, but:

- persisted
- dormant
- embodied / active
- scheduled for embodiment

The ship's society treats dormancy as normal even though individual humans may experience it very differently.
