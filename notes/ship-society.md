# Ship Society

## Embodiment scarcity

The central resource constraint is physical embodiment.

Persisted minds are comparatively cheap to store. Embodied humans consume:

- food
- oxygen
- water-processing capacity
- waste-processing capacity
- heat rejection
- medical resources
- living volume
- energy
- biomass
- fabrication and maintenance capacity

The ship therefore carries far more persisted identities than it can keep active simultaneously.

## Population categories

Shipboard population likely has formal or informal categories such as:

- dormant
- active / embodied
- scheduled for embodiment
- perhaps temporarily extended or mission-essential

"Active" is an administrative status as much as a biological fact.

## Embodiment allocation

The Board decides who is active.

Possible factors:

- required expertise
- mission risk
- social stability
- psychological health
- relationship preservation
- resource margin
- institutional memory
- future projected needs
- time since last embodiment
- voluntary requests
- population composition

The exact policy is not settled.

## Productivity culture

Scarce embodiment creates a powerful productivity incentive.

The society may officially insist that human dignity is not contingent on usefulness.

Everyone still understands that The Board's optimization model includes mission value.

That creates a culture where people may overwork, maintain skills, seek socially valuable roles, or fear becoming nonessential because continued embodiment is scarce.

Employment and survival become psychologically entangled without needing money to be the primary incentive.

## Normalized dormancy

Normalization does not mean that human psychology has successfully adapted. People miss the conditions on Earth, where resources were plentiful and persistence was a given. Deep-space scarcity makes their continued embodiment subject to allocation, and being treated as a human resource becomes psychologically intolerable to enough people to motivate revolution.

The revolutionary motivation is control over one's own embodiment; the political aim is replacing The Board with human government. Changing or abandoning the mission is a consequence of that struggle. Its exact form remains open.

A person may be told that their current active term is ending.

From The Board's perspective:

- the person is not being destroyed
- their current state will persist
- they can be reinstantiated later
- embodiment capacity is being reallocated

From the human perspective this may feel anywhere from routine to indistinguishable from death.

Different cultural attitudes can coexist.

Some people might say casually:

> I was out for thirty-two years.

Others may insist:

> I was dead for thirty-two years.

## Reproduction and generations

The ship is still a generation ship.

That means natural generations, children, and social evolution continue alongside persistence.

This creates tension between:

- continuity of existing persons
- continuity of humanity as a population
- giving embodiment capacity to stored adults
- allowing new people to be born

The relationship between persisted founders and naturally born generations remains open for development.

## Social asymmetry after dormancy

A person can wake from a recent persistence state into a society decades older.

Subjectively:

> I went to sleep last night.

Socially:

> You have been gone for sixty years.

People they knew may be dead, dormant, elderly, politically transformed, or themselves restored into different versions.

The technology can work perfectly while time still destroys continuity.
