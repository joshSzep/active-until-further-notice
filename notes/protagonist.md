# Protagonist

## Current concept

The protagonist is a woman whose final accessible persistence state is much older than the most recent version of her identity.

She wakes after a long dormancy.

Her persistence state itself is not ancient or degraded. It was current when created, likely from an ordinary night's sleep.

What is old is the **world relative to her**.

A catastrophe later made more recent persistence states inaccessible.

The later version of the protagonist lived for decades after the state from which the current protagonist is restored.

## Opening status

Upon waking, she expects an ordinary embodiment term.

Instead she learns:

- she has been dormant for a very long time
- her more recent persistence history is inaccessible
- The Board has designated her **Active Until Further Notice**

Indefinite embodiment is unusual and expensive.

Other people may see this as extraordinary privilege.

She sees it as evidence that something is badly wrong.

## Why she matters

The working idea is that this older version is uniquely positioned to undo the catastrophe that made later persistence states inaccessible.

The exact technical reason is not yet settled.

Possibilities should remain open until the catastrophe is designed.

The Board gives her an explanation that may be true but incomplete:

> Your expertise profile has become disproportionately valuable to current mission objectives.

## Social dislocation

The protagonist wakes into a world where people may know a later version of her that she never experienced being.

Someone may:

- recognize her immediately
- grieve while looking at her
- love her
- resent her
- blame her for decisions she does not remember
- know intimate things about her that she has never lived

The Board treats the identity as continuous.

Humans may not.

## The later version

If the inaccessible archive is restored, a much later persistence state of the protagonist could become available.

At first, the protagonist may think of that state as simply "me."

Her motivation could partly be:

> If I fix this, I get my life back.

But while working on the problem she accumulates new experiences, relationships, memories, and identity.

By the time the later state becomes recoverable, the question changes:

> Do I still want her to wake?

If both versions can exist, the identity tree forks.

The later version has decades of memories the protagonist lacks.

The current protagonist has the entire new life she built while solving the crisis.

Neither is a fake.

## Open details

Not settled:

- name
- age/body age at waking
- original profession
- exact skill that makes her uniquely useful
- duration of dormancy
- length of missing later lifetime
- family and relationship history
- whether she was famous, infamous, loved, or politically significant in her later version
- whether her later version helped cause the catastrophe
