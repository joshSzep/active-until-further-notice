# World Building

This note collects the broader setting around persistence, embodiment, Earth society, and the ship. Some elements are close to canon; others are still exploratory.

## Earth before the ship

Persistence technology exists long before the generation ship launches.

By launch time, society has already had decades or centuries to normalize the idea that a person's mental state can be captured, stored, and reconstructed into another body.

The generation ship is therefore not humanity's first encounter with reconstructed identity. It is a new environment that pushes an already mature technology into unfamiliar social and political extremes.

## Daily persistence

The normal persistence cadence is daily, during sleep.

A successful persistence captures the person's current mental state closely enough that accidental biological death usually means losing hours rather than years of memory.

This changes ordinary risk.

Likely Earth-side norms:

- dangerous surgery or hazardous work may trigger an extra persistence beforehand
- persistence failures are treated as serious continuity risks
- long periods without persistence are culturally uncomfortable
- after an accident, "When was their last persistence?" is a normal practical question
- sleep is partly biological rest and partly a continuity-maintenance event

Persistence should feel infrastructural and mundane rather than mystical.

## Death after persistence

Persistence divides "death" into categories that older societies did not need.

Possible distinctions:

- **biological death**: the current body dies
- **recoverable death**: a valid recent persistence state exists
- **continuity loss**: some period of lived memory is permanently lost
- **terminal death**: no viable persistence state remains

The exact terminology is not settled.

Destroying or corrupting someone's persistence archive may be treated much more severely than destroying an embodied instance.

## Legal identity

Earth society probably had to solve the practical identity problem without ever settling the metaphysical one.

A validated persistence lineage is likely treated as one continuing legal identity for purposes such as:

- property
- citizenship
- contracts
- debt
- employment
- marriage
- inheritance
- criminal and civil responsibility

The law can say "same legal person" even if philosophers, religions, and individuals disagree about whether continuity of consciousness actually survives reconstruction.

## Branching identities

Simultaneous valid versions create a harder problem.

If one version is reconstructed because the original is believed dead, and the original later survives, both share the same history up to the branch point.

Possible social solutions include:

- one-active-instance rules
- automatic legal branching after simultaneous embodiment
- heavily regulated duplicate instantiation
- explicit continuity-chain ownership rules

This remains unresolved.

The ship may inherit legal conventions from Earth, then discover that long dormancy and archive recovery make those conventions inadequate.

## Bodies and embodiment

Persisted minds are cheap compared with bodies.

The exact body-production technology is not yet settled, but it should be materially expensive enough that embodiment remains a meaningful resource constraint.

Possible requirements include:

- biomass
- growth or fabrication time
- medical capacity
- energy
- sterile facilities
- neurological integration
- rehabilitation

The technology should not make embodiment feel instantaneous or free.

## Earth versus ship economics

On Earth, mature industrial civilization may have enough resources that continuous embodiment is common, at least in wealthy societies.

The ship cannot rely on planetary-scale abundance.

Every embodied person consumes a closed-system budget:

- oxygen
- food
- water processing
- waste processing
- thermal capacity
- living volume
- medical support
- energy
- biomass

This is why a technology associated with practical immortality on Earth becomes a population-allocation system aboard ship.

## The generation ship

The vessel carries many more persisted identities than active bodies.

It remains a true generation ship:

- children are born
- generations grow up aboard
- culture changes
- people die biologically
- persisted people may return after long dormant intervals

This creates two overlapping kinds of continuity:

1. continuity through descendants
2. continuity through re-instantiation of previous people

Those systems can compete for the same embodiment resources.

## Dormancy culture

Dormancy is normalized, but not emotionally uniform.

Some people may treat it as:

> I was out for thirty years.

Others may experience it as:

> I died, and someone with my memories woke up thirty years later.

The society does not need consensus.

That disagreement should remain alive beneath ordinary language and institutions.

## Embodiment terms

Active embodiment is likely allocated in terms.

Possible patterns:

- fixed embodiment windows
- project-based embodiment
- family-overlap windows
- medical extensions
- mission-essential extensions
- indefinite status in rare cases

An indefinite designation is unusual enough to be meaningful.

The phrase **Active Until Further Notice** is therefore both bureaucratic and ominous.

## Work and status

Scarce embodiment creates a productivity culture even if money is not central.

People know that The Board considers:

- skills
- redundancy
- institutional knowledge
- psychological effects on others
- social cohesion
- mission needs
- resource cost

Officially, human worth may not be defined by productivity.

In practice, usefulness can affect whether someone remains embodied.

This creates existential pressure to stay relevant.

## The Board as institution

The Board is a distributed AI governance system, not a single personality.

It behaves more like a highly capable corporate board or executive institution.

Most decisions are presented as unified outcomes.

Internal disagreement is usually hidden, but the Board may strategically disclose dissent when doing so serves governance, legitimacy, or behavior change.

Humans may speak of constituent personas as though they are board members even if the underlying systems overlap heavily.

## Language

Corporate and administrative language should permeate the culture.

Possible terms:

- active
- dormant
- embodiment allocation
- active term
- extension
- deallocation
- mission critical
- essential personnel
- continued service
- active population
- embodiment window
- resource variance
- Board decision
- dissent recorded
- minority report

The language should often sound ordinary to people aboard.

Its horror comes from what those phrases mean, not from characters constantly pointing out how horrifying they are.

## Religion and philosophy

Persistence technology does not settle spiritual questions.

Possible cultural responses:

- strict materialists who see reconstruction as obvious survival
- continuity skeptics who regard every reconstruction as a new person
- religions that treat persistence as preserving information but not soul
- religions that incorporate persistence into existing ideas of rebirth or resurrection
- people who avoid the question because ordinary life requires practical assumptions

The Board itself remains strictly materialist and considers the metaphysical debate operationally irrelevant.

## Relationships across dormancy

Long dormant gaps distort intimacy.

A person may wake to find that:

- a spouse lived decades without them
- a child is now older than their embodied parent
- friends remember later versions they do not
- someone fell in love with a later incarnation
- grievances and reconciliations happened after their persistence point
- everyone else experienced a history they never lived

These asymmetries should be normal enough that society has etiquette around them.
